---
name: Financial monitoring purpose
description: Intended audience and scope for repurposing this mirrored military-news app.
---

Use the framework of this mirrored military-news and social-media monitoring app for financial news that military users would be interested in: financial developments that could contribute to global or domestic instability. Include prioritized news sources, social accounts, and key graphs that compare the latest observation with surrounding days, such as Fear & Greed.

**Why:** The user stated this product direction; relevance is financial instability and its security implications, not general investing.

**How to apply:** Evaluate proposed sources, ranking criteria, social rosters, and indicators against that audience and purpose. This is the intended direction for this mirror, not permission to modify a separate original app.

The financial dashboard is for a restricted internal team.

**Why:** The user explicitly selected this audience.

**How to apply:** Plan private access (including development data endpoints) and evaluate licenses for internal multi-user display, retained history, and derived summaries; do not assume either public redistribution or personal-use licensing.

For the initial version, use public sources and services already connected to this app, specifically the existing Dataminr connection, before considering new subscriptions.

**Why:** After evaluating paid-feed options, the user asked to see how far public sources and existing connections can get.

**How to apply:** Do not treat the earlier paid-feed evaluation as approval to purchase a service or alter shared Dataminr watchlists. Distinguish direct social collection from social reports arriving through existing alerts.

The dashboard should explain important world economic conditions as a PhD economist would explain them to a layperson or military official, rather than primarily displaying a ranked news list. Include current market rates, oil prices and price changes, stability comparisons over previous months, and signals from futures markets; news is a supporting section.

**Why:** The user explicitly clarified the intended dashboard experience after reviewing the news-first version.

**How to apply:** Prioritize economic context and understandable explanations when deciding which information deserves prominence. Do not treat a longer story list as a substitute for the requested economic dashboard.

The user wants a large-monitor dashboard with "Today's BLUF" across the top and five columns beneath it: U.S. financial news, world financial news, graphed key financial metrics, commodity changes, and key financial social posts. Keep clickable additional screens and the chatbot.

**Why:** The user refined the briefing into separate columns, using the original military dashboard as the layout reference.

**How to apply:** Keep independent scrolling columns on wide monitors rather than stacking every detailed panel. Supporting screens provide deeper market, economic, news and source information.

The BLUF should contain facts and explain why they matter to someone who is not well versed in finance or economics, without sounding patronizing. Do not mention missing information in the BLUF.

**Why:** The user explicitly corrected technical data-gap language appearing in the executive briefing.

**How to apply:** Keep dated observations and concise economic/security significance in the narrative. Put missing-data and source-quality notices in the supporting charts and details instead.
