That reasoning is a really good instinct — and it lines up with how the system actually works under the hood. Let me answer it directly, because there's one piece you may not realize that makes your idea even more valuable than it sounds.

Short answer: yes, and it would specifically fix the clustering problem
Here's the key thing. Priority score isn't just for ranking the feed — it already decides which items even get considered for clustering.

When the system tries to group related stories, it doesn't look at everything. It takes the top ~140 items by priority score and sends those to the AI for clustering (plus some "rescue" slots to claw back items it might've missed). Everything below that line only gets clustered if a safety-net rule happens to catch it.

So today you have a vicious cycle:

Scores are assigned per-batch, in isolation — each item graded on its own, batches scored an hour apart never compared to each other.
That makes scores bunch at 90 and become basically non-comparable across the pool.
So "top 140 by priority" is close to a coin flip among a pile of items all sitting at 90.
Which means two articles about the same event can land on opposite sides of that cutoff and never meet in the clustering pass — so they show up as separate rows instead of one clustered story.
A lot of complex "rescue" machinery exists purely to patch this. It's a band-aid over inconsistent scoring.

So your idea is the right root-cause fix
What you're describing — look at all available items holistically and score them relative to each other — would do exactly what you want:

Scores become comparable, because every item is judged against the same set instead of its little batch.
The clustering candidate pool gets smart. "Top 140" would actually mean the 140 most important things, so related items reliably land in the same pass and cluster faster.
The feed becomes intentional — a real spread (a handful of 90s, more in the 60s–70s) instead of a wall of 90s, so the ordering means something.
It can absolutely scope to the full live pool, not just what's clearing the Top Stories bar — that's the whole point.

The honest tradeoffs (so you can decide later)
It's a new recurring AI pass, not a tweak. You'd re-score the whole live pool on a schedule (or right before clustering), which is bigger/more expensive AI calls than today's per-batch scoring. Manageable with windowing, but real.
Stability vs. freshness. Pure relative scoring means an item's number shifts as its neighbors change. You'd want guardrails so the feed doesn't reshuffle every few minutes (this has to play nice with the existing time-decay and your manual pins).
It stays Curated-only. This all lives in the isolated curated path, so nothing here leaks into the main feed — same as today.
So: yes, it's feasible, and it's the right lever — it attacks the cause (inconsistent absolute scoring) instead of the symptom (manual pinning one item at a time).
